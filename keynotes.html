Simon King

Professor of Speech Processing • University of Edinburgh, UK

Keynote Speaker
Keynote Talk

Standing on the shoulders of giants

Abstract

It is often said that we are "standing on the shoulders of giants". I certainly feel that way: everything I do builds on the work of others, often from long ago. But I have observed a trend of narrowing citation patterns in our field. Too many researchers cite only very recent work. Are they really not reading "old" work? Do they think it is no longer relevant? In this talk, I will present a case study which looks not only at the methods being used and the novel contributions being claimed, but also the research questions being asked. I will convince you that the literature is a gold mine, full of great ideas and inspirations. Whatever problem you are working on, there is an "old" paper that will help you.

About the Speaker

Simon King has been with the Centre for Speech Technology Research, University of Edinburgh, since 1993, where he is now Professor of Speech Processing and Director of the Centre. His research interests span all aspects of synthetic speech generation and signal processing. He was Technical Chair of Interspeech 2023, has been an Associate Editor of the IEEE/ACM Transactions on Audio, Speech, and Language Processing, and is currently an Associate Editor of Computer Speech and Language. He is a Fellow of IEEE and a Fellow of ISCA. He directs, and teaches on, a long-running Masters programme in Speech & Language Processing, where he enjoys making students read some really "old" publications.

Daniela Claro

Full Professor • Federal University of Bahia, Brazil

Keynote Speaker
Keynote Talk

Toward a Talking Linguistic Atlas: A Roadmap for Speaking Dialect Maps of Brazilian Portuguese from ALiB Project

Abstract

The Linguistic Atlas of Brazil (ALiB) project has collected regional spoken Portuguese, which its web system, ALiBWeb, now organizes into a database that links audio, georeferenced data and linguistic charts. This talk proposes a roadmap from today's maps to a "talking atlas", where users can hear and eventually interact with regional speech. We discuss the main challenges, including legacy field-recording quality, transcription-audio alignment, data scarcity per locality and ethical use of speakers' voices.

About the Speaker

Daniela Barreiro Claro is a Full Professor at UFBA and founder of FORMAS (Research Center on Data and Natural Language). She coordinated the development of ALiBWeb, the web system of the Linguistic Atlas of Brazil (ALiB) project, from 2014 to 2022. Her research covers Natural Language Processing, Open Information Extraction for Portuguese, semantic and pragmatic interoperability, and computational approaches to dialectology. She is vice-chair of the SBC Special Committee on NLP, a member of the editorial board of the Journal of the Brazilian Computer Society (JBCS), and a member of the INCT on Responsible AI for Computational Linguistics (TILD-IAR).
